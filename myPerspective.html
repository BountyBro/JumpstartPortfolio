My Perspective

What Did I Learn?

Synopsis

Over the course of this quarter, I gradually came to understand the scope of a larger project and the signals raised to identify when to add important features, pivot current foci to meet deadlines, and when to take a step back and review the bigger picture. As a result of this experience, I now have the experience needed to more accurately assess projects of this scale.

Planning Without Experience

During the first two weeks of the quarter, I set out a comprehensive plan with features, milestones, and scheduled events. I expressed concerns with my instructor that the project would be far too small in scope to last two quarters despite the number of unknowns, and that I would need to pursue many stretch goals raised by my peers.

Seeking Direction And Pivoting

Once I began developing; however, I came to understand how much time goes into planning, communicating, presenting, and learning the very material I am trying to implement. After wrapping up a component, I found myself without motivation, having no direction on where to go next. Scheduled events began to arrive while the progress they depended upon was not remotely ready. Recognizing this, I chose to pivot from working on a non-critical, but unknown facet, to focus on the time-sensitive task at hand.

Recognizing Final Scope

Now, after many weeks of toiling over syntax and meeting with friends, I have come to understand that this project, which I initially believed would not last one quarter, would last well over two and beyond. Though I considered features from a birds-eye view, there are many complementary parts to the features that I failed to consider. Even further still, additional tools to meet user needs would be very well received and an important part of the process that I have yet to consider.

This is all to say, I have come to understand how a project that can seem small from a simpler perspective can expand to many times its perceived size
